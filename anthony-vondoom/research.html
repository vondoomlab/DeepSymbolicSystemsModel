Policy
Policy Paper  ·  30 pages
A Fixed Claim on a Shrinking Supply: AI Data Centres, Water Stress and Lock-In
AI data centres account for roughly 0.14% of global freshwater withdrawals — far less than farming or leaking pipes. The real risk is where they are being built: more than half of the world’s data-centre hubs sit in high water-stress regions, and their location fixes their water source for decades. This paper maps the evidence, examines the Nile basin as a case study, and proposes three tracks to reduce lock-in.

Policy Paper  ·  21 pages
Sri Lanka’s Social Media Ban Won’t Stop Predators
Parliament’s minimum-age law checks a birthdate, not a person. This paper uses complaint data, investigative reporting, and Australia’s own evaluation of a comparable ban to show why it fails — and what would actually close the loop: prosecution of adults and verified-identity accountability rather than age gates that any child can bypass.

From Bench to Batch: Scale-up and Commercialisation of a Chilled Ready-Meal Sauce System
Moving a recipe from a 1 kg bench batch to a 112.5 kg kettle batch changes the physics more than the arithmetic. This handbook carries one demonstration recipe through both steps: Part A covers scale-factor mathematics, heating-surface geometry, evaporation, cooling time and cost per portion; Part B applies five commercialisation gates, a full HACCP plan, and the shelf-life rules that decide which heat process a sauce needs.

Research Paper  ·  Peer-Reviewed
Why We Cooked: Food Preference, Accidental Discovery, and What the Evidence Cannot Yet Explain
Cooking is usually explained by what it gave hominins — more energy, smaller guts, larger brains. Why hominins cooked at all receives less attention. This paper reviews the ape preference evidence, makes the case for deliberate repetition of an accidental discovery, and sets out an experiment to settle what current data leaves open.
