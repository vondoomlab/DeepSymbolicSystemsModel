My Story

From Piliyandala to the World

I was born in Sri Lanka and grew up in Piliyandala, where I attended Somaweera Chandrasiri Vidyalaya. A scholarship from the Grade 5 exam took me to Nalanda College in Colombo, and during those years I also earned a diploma in graphic design. As the civil war intensified, my father decided the family should join him in Sirmione, a beautiful town on Lake Garda in Italy.

I arrived at 16 speaking only English and Sinhala. On our second day, as we walked through the old town, we met Beppe Bignotti, who already knew my father from the nearby restaurant where he worked as a chef de partie. Beppe offered me some extra shifts as an apprentice. I started the next day and enjoyed it, but my mother didn’t like me working until 23:00, so Beppe hired me as an apprentice storekeeper instead, and I took night classes to learn Italian.

From there, things grew. Beppe’s son Angelo asked his father to let me learn pizza-making, and at 17 or 18 I began working alongside him. In the evenings I attended middle school in Rivoltella and earned my licenza media, which opened the door to high school in Desenzano. Once my Italian was solid, I left school to join the Bignotti family’s gelateria full time as an apprentice gelato maker. Without realising it, I was being trained in fermentation and flavour profiles, and that training has served me ever since.

After the gelateria, I moved to a boat rental company outside Sirmione castle, where I ran the front desk, managed a fleet and built a team. Then came Princess Cruises, then Nikki Beach in Sardinia, and then a recruiting agency offered me a stage in Egypt with Veratour. I accepted without hesitation, because I had always wanted to live there. In Egypt I visited many historic sites, and I finally began writing down my research.

I am now an independent researcher in cognitive archaeology and the creator of the Deep Symbolic Systems Model (DSSM), a framework for how societies stabilise shared practices into rituals, institutions and, eventually, monuments and writing. My corpus has more than 100 open-access papers across OSF, Zenodo, SSRN and Academia.edu, along with three peer-reviewed publications in journals of Egyptian universities.

Research

The Deep Symbolic Systems Model

The DSSM is a framework for understanding how human societies stabilise shared practices — cooking, ritual, architecture, writing — into the symbolic infrastructure that holds a civilisation together across generations. It grew out of fieldwork in Egypt and has since been tested across five civilisations.

Family Emblem

Fide et Commercio

I am currently researching my ancestors. The surname I was born with — Anthonam Hettige — is a Sinhala house name built from two parts. Anthony Vondoom is the pen name and publishing alias I write under. The research is into the original name: Anthonam, a Christian baptismal name derived from the Latin Antonius, taken by ancestors navigating Portuguese and Dutch colonial rule in Sri Lanka; and Hettige, meaning “of the Hetti” — the Sinhala form of Chetty, the merchant caste. Dutch-era land registers, a 1954 Ceylon Government Gazette, and current genealogical records document these elements together. The full line is still being traced.

The parallel with my own life is not subtle. My ancestors crossed the Indian Ocean for trade, navigated four colonial regimes, changed their names to survive them, and held two identities at once — the merchant and the baptised Christian — encoding both in a surname that survived into the present. I left Sri Lanka at sixteen, crossed into Italian kitchens speaking no Italian, moved between countries and trades, and built a life that is also, at its core, about commerce and faith: faith in the work, commerce in the food. The motto was theirs first. It fits.

The emblem was made to hold that story in a single image. The research behind it is ongoing.

Arms — The Shield
The Ship, the Cross, and the Scales
At the centre of the oval shield, a sailing ship moves across waves — the emblem of a merchant family whose trade routes crossed the Indian Ocean from the Coromandel Coast to the western ports of Ceylon. Below the ship, a Latin cross stands between a pair of balanced scales. The cross encodes the Christian baptismal name Anthonam, adopted under Portuguese rule and carried forward. The scales encode Hettige: the merchant’s instrument, the measure of honest commerce, and the Chetty identity that the name carries into the present.
Dexter Supporter
The Tiger
The tiger stands to the left of the shield, rampant and jewelled — banded at the gorget and armleted in gold. In Sri Lankan and South Asian heraldic tradition, the tiger represents strength, sovereignty, and territorial origin. Here it anchors the emblem in the island: the Sinhala side of an identity that absorbed Portuguese names, Dutch registers, and English administration without losing its root.
Sinister Supporter
The Ornate Lion with Sword
To the right stands a lion whose body is covered in Eastern scrollwork, holding an upright sword. It is not a European heraldic lion; the ornamentation is drawn from South Asian decorative tradition. The sword it carries is both martial and judicial — the authority of the merchant who could defend his contracts. Together the two supporters frame a family that stood between worlds: the tiger rooted in Ceylon, the ornate lion facing outward toward trade.
Crest
The Mace, the Turban, and the Lotus
Above the shield, a mace crosses behind a bejewelled turban — authority and Eastern dress, the visible markers of a merchant family of standing. Beside them, a lotus flower: in Buddhist and Hindu symbolic tradition, the lotus grows through still water and surfaces untouched, a figure for dignity maintained through difficult passage. It is the appropriate crest for a family that moved through four colonial regimes and came out the other side still traceable.
Fide et Commercio By Faith and Commerce

Four words that compress the double identity the surname carries: Fide — the faith encoded in Anthonam, the Christian baptismal name; Commercio — the commerce encoded in Hettige, the merchant house. The research into the full family line is ongoing.
